## Module 1 — Who we are, who you talk to
**Who we drive.** Maz Services drives kids who need extra help to and from school.
**Our partners.** We work with two partner companies: FirstAlt and EverDriven. They send us the rides.
**Two people you talk to.** You only talk to two people about a ride: Maz dispatch, and the partner's dispatch. No one else.
You never call a parent. You never call the school. That is not your job.
**Partner dispatch may call you.** FirstAlt or EverDriven dispatch might call you directly about a ride. Answer the call and do what they say.
**Then tell Maz.** After you talk to the partner's dispatch, call or message Maz dispatch too. Keep us in the loop.
**Need dispatch?** Call the dispatch number (placeholder until the dedicated line is built).
**Pay questions.** Questions about your pay or your rate go to Z. Not to dispatch.
**Why this matters.** Clear phone lines keep every ride safe. Everyone knows exactly who to call.

## Module 2 — Getting onboarded
You already did most of this to get here. This is a quick map of the whole path, so you understand each piece.
**FirstAlt: step 1.** You get an invite link from FirstAlt to start your file.
**FirstAlt: step 2.** FirstAlt runs your background check. Have your last 7 years of addresses and your last 3 years of work ready — they ask for both.
**FirstAlt: step 3.** You sign a drug test consent form. Then Priority Solutions calls to book your drug test at Concentra, before your first ride.
**FirstAlt: step 4.** You take the FirstAlt online class.
**FirstAlt: step 5.** You upload your license, registration, insurance, and photos of your vehicle. Every document needs its expiry date on it.
**FirstAlt: step 6.** You sign the Acumen contract, take this course, then sign the Maz contract.
**EverDriven track.** EverDriven runs a different path. Here it is, step by step.
**EverDriven: step 1.** You register in the Contractor Compliance app and upload your license, registration, and insurance.
**EverDriven: step 2.** You get fingerprinted for a background check and take an in-person drug and alcohol test.
**EverDriven: step 3.** Your vehicle passes a 50-point inspection, and a mechanic signs off on it.
**EverDriven: step 4.** You take the Hallo English test — about 10 minutes, 5 speaking questions.
**EverDriven: step 5.** You take the SafeRide online safety course — about 4.5 hours.
**EverDriven: step 6.** You set up your banking, install the EverDriven driver app, then pick up your vest and window sticker.
**You are your own business.** You are paid as a 1099 contractor, not an employee. No taxes are taken out of your pay.
**What that means.** You owe your own taxes on what you earn. Set some of every paycheck aside for tax time.
**The W-9 form.** You fill out a form called a W-9. Pick "Individual/sole proprietor" unless you have an LLC. After the year ends, you get a 1099 form showing what you earned.

## Module 3 — Your vehicle and gear
**Registration, insurance, inspection.** Keep all three current. If one expires, you cannot drive until it's fixed. No exceptions.
**Why it matters.** An expired document can end a ride before it starts, and it can cost you routes.
**Vest and placard.** Wear your vest. Put your placard in the window. Every ride, every day.
**Wheelchair rides.** Some vans need a driver who is approved for wheelchairs. Never take a wheelchair ride unless you are that approved driver.
**Camera, if you have one.** Some drivers have a partner-issued camera in their van. If you were given one, keep it on for every ride.
**No camera? That's fine.** Not every driver has a camera. If you don't have one, you don't need one.
**Keep the car clean.** A clean car is part of the job. Trash, smells, and clutter are not okay with a child in the car.
**No smoking, no vaping.** Never smoke or vape in the car — not before a ride, not between rides. No smoke smell, ever.
**Only the assigned rider.** No other passengers, ever. Not your kids, not your friends, not another driver. Only the student on your route.
**Why.** Every rider is checked in and tracked. An extra person in the car is not allowed and is not safe.
**Example.** Your spouse needs a ride and it's right on your way. Still no — the car is for the assigned student only, every time.

## Module 4 — Reading your ride
A ride has a name, like "Overlake IB 02 (W)". Each part tells you something.
**IB and OB.** IB means inbound — the ride to school. OB means outbound — the ride home. Both legs happen every day.
**The number.** The number is the student's number. Same number, same student, all year long.
**Example.** "Overlake IB 02" and "Overlake OB 02" are the same student's morning and afternoon ride.
**The day letter.** "(W)" means this is the Wednesday version of this route. Some days run at different times.
**Early release days.** On early release days, PM pickup times change. Always check the time on the route, not just the usual time.
**Read the notes.** Every route has notes. Read them before you accept, every single time.
**What notes tell you.** Notes can mention equipment like a wheelchair, allergies, or behavior you should know about.
**Loops.** Sometimes you get several rides in a row with one driver. This is called a loop.
**Why loops matter.** Being late on the first ride of a loop puts the next ride at risk. Say something early if you're running behind.

## Module 5 — Accepting a ride
**The window.** The app lets you accept a ride about 75 minutes before pickup.
**Accept fast.** Accept the moment the ride opens. Don't wait.
**Step 1.** At 75 minutes before pickup, the ride shows up as ready to accept.
**Still waiting — 75 minute mark.** If you haven't accepted, you get a text reminder around this time.
**Still waiting — 45 minute mark.** At 45 minutes before pickup, dispatch calls you directly.
**Still waiting — 30 minute mark.** At 30 minutes before pickup, Z is alerted that the ride is still open.
**Why it matters.** Every call you get because you didn't accept is a mark against you. Too many, and you can lose routes.
**Partners are watching.** Partners can see how fast you accept. Fast acceptance builds trust.
**Example.** A ride opens at 7:00 for an 8:15 pickup. Accept it right then — don't wait until 7:45.

## Module 6 — Running the ride, step by step
**Step 1: Start.** Tap Start in the app the moment you leave for pickup.
**Step 2: drive the route.** Follow the route the app gives you. Read the notes twice if it's your first time on this route.
**Step 3: arrive.** Get to the pickup spot on time — early is on time.
**EverDriven: tap At Pickup.** When you arrive, tap At Pickup. This tells dispatch you made it. A lot of drivers skip this — don't be one of them.
**PM pickup at school.** For afternoon pickups, wait at the school pickup spot. Staff bring the student out to you.
**If the student doesn't come out.** Call dispatch. Never go inside the school. Never call the school yourself.
**No-load steps.** Wait 5 to 10 minutes — the school district sets the exact time — then call dispatch.
**No-load: what dispatch tells you.** Dispatch tells you to mark it a no-load in the app. You still get full pay.
**Wrong address or notes don't match.** Call dispatch before you move the car. Don't guess.
**Step 4: drop-off.** Only drop the student at the exact stop you were given. Never a different spot, even if asked.
**Step 5: End.** Tap End in the app while you are still at the drop-off spot. Then you can close the app.
**Why the exact spot matters.** Tapping Start or End from the wrong place can look like a fake ride, and dispatch may have to take that pay back.
**Partner dispatch may call mid-ride.** If the partner's dispatch calls you during a ride, answer and follow their directions. Then tell Maz dispatch what happened.

## Module 7 — The two apps
**FirstAlt screens.** Screens, in order: Assign, Accept, En Route, Onboard, Complete.
**What each FirstAlt screen means.** Assign is your ride waiting. Accept confirms it's yours. En Route means you're driving there. Onboard means the student is in the car. Complete means the ride is done.
**EverDriven screens.** Screens, in order: Scheduled, Accepted, Active, At Pickup, Completed.
**What each EverDriven screen means.** Scheduled is the ride waiting. Accepted confirms it's yours. Active means you're driving. At Pickup means you arrived. Completed means the ride is done.
**The tap most drivers miss.** The At Pickup tap is easy to miss. Tap it every time you arrive — it's your proof that you showed up.
**Keep the app ready.** Keep the app installed, logged in, and notifications turned on.
**Keep your phone ready.** Keep location and GPS turned on, your phone charged, and mounted where you can see it while driving.
**If the app won't start a ride.** Take a screenshot of the screen.
**Then.** Call dispatch before you drive. Never drive a ride the app can't track.
**Start and end in the zone.** Always tap Start and End while you're inside the pickup or drop-off zone, not from your driveway or down the street.

## Module 8 — The six driving rules
1. **Accept on time.** When the app offers your ride, accept it as soon as you can. See the Accepting a ride module for the exact window.
2. **Arrive on time.** Being early is on time. Being right on time is cutting it close.
3. **Straight there, straight home.** No stops — not for gas, not for coffee, not for errands. Fill your tank before your route starts.
4. **No eating while you drive.** Water and coffee are fine. No eating. The real rule: nothing that takes your eyes or your hands off driving.
**No phone in hand.** Never hold your phone while driving. Mount it, and only touch it when you're stopped.
5. **No detours.** Follow the route the app gives you. If the road is blocked, call dispatch — don't decide on your own.
6. **Never speed.** Every ride is tracked. Speeding shows up.
**Why these six.** These six rules protect the kids in your car and protect your routes.
**Example.** You're running 5 minutes late. Don't speed to make it up — call dispatch instead.
**Example.** You need coffee before your shift. Get it before you accept the ride, not on the way.

## Module 9 — The children
**Greet and settle.** Greet the child by name. Use the same seat every day if the child prefers it — routine is comfort.
**Seat belts, every time.** Every child wears a seat belt, every ride, no exceptions.
**Car seats and boosters.** If the notes say a child needs a car seat or booster, use it every time. Never skip it.
**Wheelchairs.** If a child is in a wheelchair, strap it down completely before you drive. Check it every single ride, even if it looked fine yesterday.
**If a child has a hard moment** (crying, shouting, won't stay seated): pull over somewhere safe and call dispatch.
**What you never do.** Never discipline. Never grab. Never argue. Dispatch handles it from there.
**Never leave a child alone.** Never leave a child alone in the vehicle, for any reason, for any amount of time.
**Exact stop only.** Never drop a child anywhere but the exact stop you were given, even if someone asks you to.
**Privacy.** What happens in the car stays private. No photos of children. No posts about your riders, ever.
**Why privacy matters.** These are kids, and their families trust us to keep their information safe.

## Module 10 — When something goes wrong
- **Someone is hurt or in danger:** call 911 first. Then call dispatch.
- **Anything else:** call dispatch first. Not 911.
- **Child gets sick in the car:** pull over somewhere safe, call dispatch.
- **Child unbuckles or hits:** pull over, call dispatch, and stay calm. Never grab.
- **Breakdown with a child in the car:** pull over, stay with the child, and call dispatch. Someone will come to you.
- **Accident: check everyone first.** Check that everyone is okay. Call 911 if anyone is hurt.
- **Accident: then call dispatch.** Call dispatch right after 911, or right away if no one is hurt.
- **Accident: take photos** of both cars and the scene.
- **Accident: get the other driver's info** — name, license plate, and insurance information.
- **Accident: write it down** within 24 hours. Just the facts.
- **Accident: never say whose fault.** That's not your call to make.
- **Sick or can't drive:** call dispatch the second you know — even at 5am.
- **Running late on one ride of a loop:** say so before the next ride is at risk.
- **Snow day or a closure:** dispatch will tell you. Never drive to a closed school.
- **Late in general:** call dispatch before pickup time is due, not after.

## Module 11 — How you get paid
**Pay is per ride.** Pay is set per ride and per route by Z. It's not per hour, and it's not per mile.
**Wheelchair pay.** Wheelchair routes pay more.
**No pay between rides.** There's no pay for driving between rides.
**Weekly pay.** You get paid weekly.
**When the money lands.** Rides you drive Monday to Friday are paid on the Friday two weeks later.
**Direct deposit.** Pay comes by direct deposit.
**Under $100 carries over.** If you earn under $100 in a week, it's not lost. It carries to the next week.
**Your stub.** Your stub comes by email. It shows the week, your rides, your total, anything held back, and any carried balance.
**What "held back" means.** Held back means Z is holding money for a reason she'll explain — for example, a ride that got paid twice by mistake gets taken back the next week.
**Example.** In May, a driver was paid twice for the same ride. The extra pay was taken back the next cycle, and the stub showed it as held back.
**If you spot a mistake.** Reply to the stub email or message Z. Give her the ride date and the route name.
**Mistakes get fixed** the next pay cycle.
**Who to ask.** Pay and rate questions go to Z. Not to dispatch.

## Module 12 — Cancellations, no-shows, no-loads
**Late cancel.** Ride cancelled 1 to 2 hours before it starts: you still get full pay.
**No-load wait.** Student not there? Wait 5 to 10 minutes — the school district sets the exact time.
**No-load steps.** Call dispatch. Dispatch tells you to mark it a no-load in the app.
**No-load pay.** You still get full pay for a no-load.
**Driver no-show.** You don't show up for a ride: no pay, and a talk about your contract.
**Cancel the night before.** If the ride is cancelled the night before, nothing is owed to you — and you'll be told.
**Example.** Your ride is at 7:30am. At 7:15, the partner cancels it. You still get full pay.
**Example.** You wait 8 minutes and no student comes out. You call dispatch, they tell you to mark it a no-load. Full pay.

## Module 13 — Your scorecard
**Six numbers.** Every week you get six numbers: acceptance, on-time start, on-time arrival, on-time completion, responsiveness, reliability.
**Acceptance.** How fast and how often you accept rides.
**On-time start, arrival, completion.** Whether you started, arrived, and finished each ride on time.
**Responsiveness.** How fast you answer when dispatch calls or messages you.
**Reliability.** Whether dispatch can count on you, ride after ride.
**Why it matters.** Drivers dispatch never has to chase get the most routes.
**Low numbers** mean fewer routes, then a conversation.
**Partners see it too.** Partners can also remove a driver from their routes.
**No ranks.** There are no ranks or labels. Just your six numbers.

## Module 14 — Staying current
**What expires.** Your registration, insurance, inspection, drug test, and background check all expire.
**Reminders** come from dispatch, or from Z.
**Renew on time.** Renew the week you're told. Not the last day.
**If something expires:** no driving until it's fixed. No exceptions.
**Send it in.** Send the new document to Z the same day you get it.
**Why it's strict.** An expired document can put a ride, and your routes, on hold.
**Example.** Your insurance renews on the 1st. If you're told to renew it that week, don't wait until the 28th.
**Plan ahead.** Check your dates now so nothing catches you by surprise.
